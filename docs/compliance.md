# Record checks and their sources

`npm run park -- compliance` (the `/compliance` command) checks the records against the rules below. Each check is a query in the `v_compliance` view in `supabase/migrations/0001_holiday_park.sql`, and the GST worked out on each casual stay is in the `v_night_gst` and `v_booking_gst` views. A clean result means the records are complete and consistent. It does not mean the park meets the law: that is your call, with your lawyer and your accountant.

Sources were read on 1 October 2026. Rules change. If a source says something different from this page, the source wins: update this page and the check together (`/customise`).

| Rule | What the data must show | Source |
|---|---|---|
| `PARK-REGISTRATION` | The camping-ground certificate of registration (New Zealand) or the caravan park registration or licence (Australia) has its number and expiry date in settings, and is not within 45 days of expiry or past it. In New Zealand land may not be used as a camping ground without a current certificate of registration from the local authority, councils renew it every year, and it is an offence to operate without one. | [Camping-Grounds Regulations 1985](https://www.legislation.govt.nz/regulation/public/1985/0261/latest/whole.html), for example [Masterton District Council: camping ground registration](https://www.mstn.govt.nz/rules-regulations-and-licenses/business-licenses/camping-ground-registration) |
| `NZ-TENANCY-50-DAYS` | Every long-stay agreement past `nz_tenancy_days` (default 50) days, and every permanent resident, has a decision recorded on which law covers it (`tenancy_law`: tenancy, park or excluded). The Residential Tenancies Act 1986 does not apply to a cabin, caravan, tent or other structure in a camping ground regulated under the Health Act 1956 that is designed for occupation for periods not exceeding 50 days in a continuous term (s 5(1)(ta)), nor, on conditions, to bare land rented to place your own caravan or shelter (s 5(1)(t)) or a self-contained caravan or relocatable home where the stay is agreed to be temporary or transient (s 5(1)(tb), s 5(3)). Past those limits, or where the park is someone's home, the Act may apply. The check also lists casual bookings longer than 50 nights so they can be set up as agreements. | [Residential Tenancies Act 1986, section 5](https://www.legislation.govt.nz/act/public/1986/0120/latest/whole.html), [Tenancy Services: who tenancy law does not protect](https://www.tenancy.govt.nz/starting-a-tenancy/who-is-protected/who-tenancy-law-does-not-protect/) |
| `VIC-RESIDENT-60-DAYS` | In Victoria (settings `country` AU, `state` VIC), an occupant who lives in the park as their main residence with the owner's permission and has been there 60 days or more without a break is a resident under the Residential Tenancies Act 1997. The check asks, for every such agreement, for the date the pre-agreement disclosure was given (whether the park is to be sold, flooding, subsidence, the electricity supply) and the date the park rules were given. | [Consumer Affairs Victoria: residency in a caravan park](https://www.consumer.vic.gov.au/housing/renting/starting-and-changing-rental-agreements/different-rental-agreements/residency-in-a-caravan-park) |
| `NSW-OCCUPATION-AGREEMENT` | In New South Wales (`state` NSW), every annual (long-term casual) agreement is in writing, has a disclosure statement given before it was entered into (s 9: the questions and written answers on fees, extra fees in busy periods and more), and had the park rules given before or at the time it was signed (s 10). The Act covers an occupant on a casual basis for at least 12 months. | [Holiday Parks (Long-term Casual Occupation) Act 2002](https://legislation.nsw.gov.au/view/whole/html/inforce/current/act-2002-088), [NSW Government: holiday parks](https://www.nsw.gov.au/housing-and-construction/community-living/holiday-parks) |
| `WRITTEN-AGREEMENT` | Every long-stay agreement has a signed agreement on file and the date it was signed. | Operator policy. It is also what the NSW and Victorian checks above build on. |
| `NZ-LONG-STAY-GST` | A camping ground is a commercial dwelling. Accommodation in a commercial dwelling carries GST at 15%; after four weeks, GST is charged on 60% of the value of the domestic goods and services, and from the start when a stay of more than four weeks is agreed up front. The reduced value does not apply when the stay is supplied through an online marketplace. The system applies 60% from night 29 (or night 1 when `long stay agreed` is set on the booking) and skips bookings on channels of kind `ota`. The check lists every casual stay over 28 nights so you can confirm the treatment. How GST applies to the site fees on long-stay agreements is not worked out here: decide it with your accountant. | [Inland Revenue: GST and commercial accommodation](https://www.ird.govt.nz/property/commercial-property/gst-and-commercial-accommodation) |
| `AU-LONG-STAY-GST` | Caravan parks and camping grounds are commercial residential premises. For long-term accommodation (28 or more continuous days, to an individual) a provider can choose to charge GST on 50% of the GST-inclusive price from the 28th day. The system applies it from night 28 only when `au_long_stay_concession` is on in settings; otherwise the check lists each long casual stay charged full GST so you can decide with your accountant. Long-stay site fees are not worked out here. | [ATO: commercial residential property](https://www.ato.gov.au/businesses-and-organisations/gst-excise-and-indirect-taxes/gst/in-detail/your-industry/property/gst-and-commercial-property/commercial-residential-property), [GSTR 2012/7](https://www.ato.gov.au/law/view/document?docid=GST/GSTR20127/NAT/ATO/00001) |
| `GST-NUMBER` | Invoices are being issued and settings has a GST number (NZ) or ABN (AU) to print on them. | [Inland Revenue: how taxable supply information works](https://www.ird.govt.nz/gst/tax-invoices-for-gst/how-tax-invoices-for-gst-work), [ATO: tax invoices](https://www.ato.gov.au/businesses-and-organisations/gst-excise-and-indirect-taxes/gst/tax-invoices) |
| `NZ-INVOICE-OVER-1000` | Every invoice over NZ$1,000 has, on the guest record, the buyer's name and at least one identifier: address, phone, email or business number. Inland Revenue requires these for a GST-registered buyer on supplies over $1,000; the check asks for them on every such invoice because the office rarely knows who is registered. | [Inland Revenue: how taxable supply information works](https://www.ird.govt.nz/gst/tax-invoices-for-gst/how-tax-invoices-for-gst-work) |
| `AU-INVOICE-1000-PLUS` | Every invoice of A$1,000 or more shows the buyer's identity or ABN. | [ATO: tax invoices](https://www.ato.gov.au/businesses-and-organisations/gst-excise-and-indirect-taxes/gst/tax-invoices) |
| `PRIVACY-RETENTION` | No guest record whose last stay ended more than `privacy_review_years` ago (default 7) is kept without a decision, unless they hold an active agreement. NZ Privacy Principle 9: an agency must not keep personal information for longer than is required for the purposes for which it may lawfully be used. Australian Privacy Principle 11.2 asks the same of APP entities. Tax records have their own keeping periods: keep the invoice, anonymise the guest. | [Privacy Commissioner: Principle 9](https://www.privacy.org.nz/privacy-principles/9/), [OAIC: Australian Privacy Principles](https://www.oaic.gov.au/privacy/australian-privacy-principles/australian-privacy-principles-quick-reference) |
| `CARD-DATA` | No guest note holds something shaped like a card number. Card numbers belong with your payment provider, not in free text. | Operator policy, with Privacy Principle 5 (storage and security) at the [Privacy Commissioner](https://www.privacy.org.nz/privacy-principles/5/) |
| `DOUBLE-BOOKING` | No site is sold twice for the same night, across your own bookings, the long-stay agreements and the outside calendars you import. | Operator policy |

## Thresholds you can change

`nz_tenancy_days` (50), `vic_resident_days` (60), `long_stay_nights` (28), `privacy_review_years` (7) and `meter_read_days` (35) are settings: `npm run park -- set settings --vic-resident-days=60`. Change one only when the source changes, and update this page in the same change.

## What the checks do not do

- They do not decide which tenancy law covers an agreement. They make sure someone has decided and written it down.
- They do not work out notice periods, rent or fee increase notice, bond limits or electricity on-charging limits. Those depend on the law the agreement falls under and the state or region. `/give-notice` records that notice was given; it does not say the notice is valid.
- They do not cover Queensland, South Australia, Western Australia, Tasmania or the territories' residential park laws, site spacing and amenity ratios under the Camping-Grounds Regulations, fire evacuation schemes, building warrants of fitness, pool safety, drinking water, or food and liquor licensing. The on-site register (`npm run docs -- on-site-register`) is a list of who is in the park, not an evacuation scheme. Add a rule with `/customise` when your park needs one, with its source.
- They read your records. A stay taken on paper and never entered is invisible to them.

Nothing here is legal or tax advice.
